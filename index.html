Connectome modelling · October 2026

Does the circuit effect hold?

I proposed and directed a study asking whether disconnecting one feeding-circuit neuron still reduces simulated motor-neuron output when recurrent connection strength changes.

In a 127,400-neuron implementation of a published fly model, setting Roundup’s outgoing weights to zero reduced MN9 spiking in all 15 paired comparisons: three connection strengths and five input seeds.

Exploratory model sensitivity: one circuit intervention, output neuron and input regime, at three selected gains. These simulated spikes do not establish a change in real feeding behaviour.

Methods, limits & evidence

All 30 scientific runs passed independent saved-output verification. Each trial lasted one simulated second. Intact and disconnected conditions shared exogenous input candidates; refractory acceptance could still differ with circuit state. Both this shared-candidate design and an execution correction were post-failure amendments, not preregistered.

Five input seeds are not five animals. No statistical significance, biological validation, novelty or full replication of the source paper is claimed. The source paper documents left-right inversion of the dataset and uses corrected biological sides. Genetic-driver coverage and historical cell-ID lineage remain unverified.

Shiu et al. (2024), source paper · Pinned source model

EEG decoding · October 2026

Does the decoding gain survive?

I proposed and directed a study testing whether time-lagged EEG features retain their advantage when normalization uses only an earlier recording session.

The lagged representation retained a 7.81 percentage-point advantage in pooled balanced accuracy over a dimension-matched duplication control on 1,050 later-session trials: positive in seven participants and tied in two.

Nine participants; evaluation recordings had already been inspected. This is an exploratory comparison, not evidence of performance on new people or an untouched test set.

Methods, limits & evidence

Exact raw-data replay covered all nine participants and both sessions. Each representation’s classifier was fitted once on 1,080 earlier-session trials and kept fixed while normalization changed. Duplicating current signals controls feature dimension without adding time-lagged information.

Balanced accuracy averages recall across the three selected classes. With earlier-session normalization, pooled scores were 45.52% for lag-4 and 37.71% for duplicated current signals; the difference was 7.94 points when participants received equal weight.

Independent saved-output verification passed after a disclosed verifier-metadata correction; no model was refitted. This comparison does not isolate a neural mechanism, establish online performance or qualify a competition submission.

Scherer et al. (2015), source dataset study · BNCI2015-004, selected numeric classes 1, 2 and 4.

Experiment notes & sources

To reproduce the demo example, leave both source strengths at 1× and remove BMP bilateral region 1 (either outermost handle). Keep all six SHH source pairs. Run the condition and compare the converged endpoints: dorsal identity changes from 48.8% to 45.6%, intermediate from 12.5% to 15.7%, and ventral stays at 38.7%. In this run, 3.2% of model tissue changes identity. These are model outputs, not measured biological effects.

The solver starts both fields at zero and stops when each field’s relative update is at most 10−4 and the decoded identity holds for five consecutive qualifying steps. A 1,000-iteration cap rejects unsettled runs. Solver iterations represent numerical relaxation, not developmental time.

The biological context includes Jessell (2000) and Zagorski et al. (2017); these support the qualitative mechanism, not the chosen decoder parameters. The independent atlas view uses CC0 population-average GW21–36 surfaces from the dHCP fetal surface atlas. The atlas is a separate data reference, not a response to the molecular intervention. Further numerical details are available in the demo’s evidence panel.

Can measurements distinguish diffusion from clearance?

Synthetic parameter recovery: a separate 1D study examined which measurements distinguish diffusion from clearance. After source shutoff with zero-flux boundaries, the total-amount time course constrains clearance but contains no diffusion information. In the noiseless seven-reading comparison, one spatial arrangement left roughly as many plausible parameter pairs as a local trace; reversing the arrangement was much less informative. Recovery benefits from knowing the generating model, source and calibration. This is a within-model result, not biological validation or a re-analysis of experimental assays.

This separate 1D experiment compares total amount, a local trace and spatial observations. It does not calibrate the public 2D demo.

Play or scrub the geometric transition.

Two 3D surfaces. No matching vertices. This prototype blends their distance fields, then extracts the shape in between.

The transition is geometric; it does not track corresponding anatomy or model biological development.

Method & source

Mesh repair → SDF sampling → interpolation → isosurface extraction. CPU and conditional PyTorch/CUDA paths. Surface quality depends on mesh repair and resolution; memory use grows cubically with grid resolution.

The recording uses Allen Mouse CCF meshes from BrainGlobe, including a dentate-gyrus-to-whole-brain transition. Image/data credit: Allen Institute. Noncommercial research demonstration. Sources and licence details.
