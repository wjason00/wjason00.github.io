SourcesArchive, movement assumptions and reuse terms

The signal display follows prescribed input into a saved brain simulation, then saved motor output into the assumed body mapping. Motor markers brighten for 40 ms after saved spikes. This replay export contains motor spike times, not sensory spike times. The paired comparison in Connections uses a separate dataset.

The Shiu et al. (2024) author simulation archive supplies sugar (100 Hz) or water (200 Hz), three circuit conditions and 30 repeats per case. MN9 and MN6 come from the same saved file and trial. Matching repeat numbers across conditions do not establish paired inputs.

MN9 is mapped to the main reach and head coupling; left and right MN6 signals drive the respective distal source joints. The source calls those parts labrum_left and labrum_right; their correspondence to feeding anatomy remains unqualified. No movement parameters were fitted to animal videos. The neural interval is 0–1 s, followed by assumed actuator relaxation.

Geometric mouth–droplet contact gates an illustrative volume change. This is not fluid physics, pumping, swallowing or a closed sensory loop. The body is not registered to the FlyWire neuron coordinates shown in the anatomy view.

FlyBody geometry is redistributed under Apache 2.0; Three.js and the source neural model retain their MIT licenses. Geometry is losslessly gzip-compressed; motor events and motor/contact policies are unchanged. Package provenance.

This is a replay of an author’s simulation archive with an assumed body mapping. It does not measure an animal eating, predict swallowed volume or feed sensory feedback into the brain.
