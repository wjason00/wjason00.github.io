01G27, a g.tec BR41N.IO team projectEEG imagery decoding

When in the trial does EEG imagery become decodable?

How to read this figure · 5 steps

  1. 01What happens in one trial

    From 0–2 s the participant gets ready. At 2 s the instruction appears (documented in the protocol), followed by 2–3.5 s of instructed imagery. From 3.5 s feedback may be on — that start time is assumed, not recorded in the data.

  2. 02Decision time & the 50% chance line

    At any decision time on the horizontal axis, a decoder sees only the 1 s of EEG ending at that moment. Each point along the curve is a separate decoder fitted to that shifted 1 s window. The horizontal dotted line at 50% is chance.

  3. 03Near chance until ~3 s, then ~80%

    Scores stay near the 50% chance line until about 3 s, then rise to around 80% once the 1 s window covers the instructed imagery period and beyond.

  4. 04Why +4.25 s beats +3.5 s (and its limit)

    Two points are marked: 82/120 at +3.5 s and 99/120 at +4.25 s. Because the +4.25 s window spans 3.25–4.25 s, it overlaps the assumed 3.5 s feedback start by 0.75 s — so the later gain may partly reflect feedback.

    Limit: both points were scored on already-inspected later training trials, making this a timing comparison rather than a held-out test gain.

  5. 05Calibration result & my role

    In a separate check using 60 early calibration trials per session, choosing between CSP and Riemannian decoders scored 75/120 against 82/120 for fixed CSP — selection did not help.

    Team project across 3 participants and 6 sessions. I contributed evaluation pull requests and obtained and relayed the organisers’ scoring rules.

Full G27 account: method, my part and limits

Question. Our team tested whether early EEG calibration data could select between common spatial patterns (CSP) and a Riemannian decoder for each session, and how decoding depends on when in the trial the decision is made.

Calibration. With 60 early calibration trials per session, the selector scored 75/120 against 82/120 for fixed CSP. Selection did not beat the fixed decoder.

Timing. Each point in the timing figure is a separate fit on a causal one-second window ending at that decision time, shifted along the trial and scored on later training trials that we had already inspected. The marked points are 82/120 at +3.5 s and 99/120 at +4.25 s. Feedback onset was assumed, not recorded, and the trials were not held out, so the difference is a timing comparison, not a held-out gain. The data cover 3 participants and 6 sessions.

Electrode layout. The electrode mapping was inferred and its left–right orientation is unresolved. The layout figure compares two candidate layouts for each of 12 recording files from 3 participants, not 12 independent participants. Its fit is a negative rank correlation, not accuracy or probability.

My part. I shared evaluation work through pull requests and obtained and relayed the organisers’ scoring rules, distinguishing their peak-time score from our fixed-time comparison. The figures are the G27 team’s, shown unchanged from the team presentation.

Fly feeding circuit: study, figures & replay

Fly feeding circuit

Does disconnecting Roundup, a circuit neuron, reduce activity in motor neuron MN9?

Connection-strength study

I proposed and directed a study of whether removing Roundup’s outputs still reduces MN9 activity when connection strength changes.

In a 127,400-neuron implementation of a published fly model, setting Roundup’s outgoing weights to zero reduced MN9 spiking in all 15 paired comparisons: three connection strengths and five input seeds.

The result concerns one motor neuron and input regime in a computational model; it does not establish a change in real feeding behaviour.

Explore the circuit comparison
Methods & sources

Thirty one-second simulations compare Roundup’s outgoing connections kept or cut at three recurrent gains and five matched input seeds.

Within each seed, both conditions at all three gains shared candidate and accepted sensory inputs. A refractory-eligibility filter rejected some candidates; accepted inputs matched across these runs. The shared-input design and an execution correction were adopted after initial failures; the design was not preregistered.

The five seeds represent alternative model inputs, not animals. The comparison covers one neuron intervention, motor output and input regime. The source paper corrects left–right labels in its dataset; genetic-driver coverage and historical cell-ID lineage are not established here.

Input timing

Does timing change the effect of cutting more connections?

I proposed and directed a comparison of cutting only Roundup’s connection to MN9 with cutting all its outputs. Across five artificial input templates, shifted timing increased, decreased or left unchanged the difference between the cuts.

No consistent direction across these five templates. This is a simulation result from one fixed model and measurement window.

Separating neural signals

Can stimulus and choice be separated when errors are rare?

When stimulus and choice usually agree, can the analysis tell their signals apart? In this synthetic study, a component can be strongly labelled as choice-related while reconstructing the known choice signal poorly. With no error trials, stimulus and choice cannot be separated by this fit.
