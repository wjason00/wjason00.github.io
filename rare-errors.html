The useful distinction.

What was measured?

Demixed principal component analysis (dPCA) uses task labels to find low-dimensional components associated with time, stimulus, choice and their interaction. Here, the generating factors are known, so attribution and reconstruction can be assessed separately.

Choice attribution is the fraction of factor-driven score energy attributable to the true choice factor in the choice-labelled branch. Choice reconstruction error is that branch’s squared error on independent held-out cell means, divided by the known pure choice signal’s energy. It is not a classification error rate. The headline multiplier divides the selected mean error by the balanced mean error, using the same five noise seeds.

The fixed design uses 24 synthetic neurons, 32 time bins, two stimuli and two choices. Each positive-count fit weights the four observed cell means equally; rare cells still have noisier estimates. Test pools and noise seeds are paired across the six allocations. At zero errors, the design has rank two and dPCA is not fitted.

The separate PCA comparison has the same four-component budget but no predefined choice branch. dPCA receives task labels; PCA does not. This view makes no claim that one method is generally better.

Scope, provenance & downloads

This independent methods study uses synthetic data with known generating factors. The controls display saved results; they do not run a new fit. Biological recordings have not been evaluated.

One population geometry, fixed signal strengths and one regularizer limit the conclusion. These curves do not establish a universal minimum error count or causal choice coding.

Method: Kobak et al. (2016), using the pinned Machens Lab Python implementation.
